Trust is the new checkout

How Visa can turn AI shopping agents from a pilot into a habit.

Kyi Sin Thant

Why this question

With real money on the line, what would make people let an AI agent pay for them?

Visa has already built the rails for AI agents to pay. I wanted to understand what it would take for people to actually use them, and what Visa's Data & AI team could do about it.

02

The short version

The rails are built. Trust is what's missing.

What's happening

AI agents have started buying for people.

  • Morgan Stanley expects $190B to $385B of US spending to run through shopping agents by 2030.
  • That's 10% to 20% of US e-commerce.
  • Visa, Mastercard, OpenAI and Google have all launched ways for agents to pay.
What I found

The revenue is modest. The position is big.

  • In my 2030 base case, about $99B of agent spending would run on Visa's network.
  • That earns Visa about $278M in net revenue, under 1% of its FY2025 net revenue of $40.0B.
  • Only 6% of shoppers would let an AI buy without checking in first.
What I recommend

Make control visible to shoppers.

  • Put agent limits and approvals inside the banking apps people already use.
  • Start with everyday baskets under $100.
  • Give every agent purchase a clear way to fix mistakes.

08

Recommendation · three steps, in order

Put the controls where people already manage their money

STEP 1 · BUILD THE CONTROLS

An "Agents" screen in every issuer's app

  • See every agent using your card
  • Set a spending limit per agent
  • Approve big buys with a passkey
  • Switch an agent off in one tap

Built on Visa's existing agent tokens and passkeys, so issuers add a screen, not a new system.

STEP 2 · LAUNCH SMALL

Start with the everyday basket

  • Groceries and household refills
  • Orders under $100
  • Repeat purchases that build the habit fast

69% of shoppers are already comfortable here. Morgan Stanley puts these categories at 19% of 2030 agent spending.

STEP 3 · BACK IT UP

A clear fix when an agent gets it wrong

  • One dispute path for every agent purchase
  • Visa's record shows what the agent was allowed to buy and what it bought

Half of shoppers would blame the AI platform today. Visa can be the neutral record that settles it.

Data & AI team's part: agent-level spending signals, risk scores for issuers, and a consent-first data policy with the Chief Privacy Officer.

10

Risks and what I'd test first

What could go wrong

Issuers move slowly

Banking app changes take time.

Test: start with two digital-first issuers and a shared design Visa provides.

Agents skip cards

Bank transfers or stablecoins could win the agent slot.

Test: keep supporting every protocol through Intelligent Commerce Connect.

Controls add friction

Too many prompts and people give up.

Test: smart default limits that need no setup, approvals only above the limit.

Privacy concerns

Sharing purchase data with agents can feel invasive.

Test: consent-first sharing, written with the Chief Privacy Officer, explained in plain words.

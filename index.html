I'm drawn to the moment AI stops being a separate tool and becomes part of how people already work.

That means smart defaults, fewer steps, and help that appears at the right time inside the apps people open every day.

For large tech platforms, that streamlining leads to more daily use, longer sessions and fewer people dropping off after they sign up.

It also produces better data, since every suggestion a user accepts or ignores shows what to build next.

I use customer research and analytics to decide which AI features are worth building, then measure whether they actually help.

LA Stay Shortlist

Which Los Angeles neighborhoods and room types should a first-time visitor shortlist for their budget and length of stay?

  • Built a Streamlit dashboard where travelers filter by budget, length of stay and room type, then see matching neighborhoods on a map with distances to Downtown, the beach and LAX.
  • Added a side-by-side view to compare two neighborhoods on price, how many places fit the budget, and reviews.
  • Listings without a price still count toward totals but are left out of price averages. Unusually high prices can be hidden with one filter.
  • Wrote a methods page that explains each number and what the data can't tell you, like cleaning fees and taxes.
  • Python
  • pandas
  • Streamlit
  • Plotly

AfterChat

Graduate students network all the time, then forget what was said and miss the follow-up. What should an app for the hours after a coffee chat do?

  • Built a working app prototype. Log a chat and it writes a summary, pulls out the advice, drafts a thank-you email and sets follow-up reminders.
  • Added job alerts that show which of your contacts could refer you, plus a searchable library of advice by industry.
  • Shaped the app around a survey of 60 graduate students. 72% had a coffee chat in the last three months, and 38% struggled to follow up on time or didn't know what to write.
  • Designed for the two biggest worries, AI accuracy (27%) and privacy (15%), by showing the original note next to every summary and keeping chats private.

  • AI features
  • UX design
  • Survey design
  • Segmentation

Jack's Customs

How many pillows, aprons and bundles should a one-person Berkeley upholstery business make each week to earn the most from 42 hours and limited materials?

  • Interviewed the owner and priced every material from supplier rolls to build a cost for each product.
  • Built an integer programming model in Excel Solver. The best plan is 24 pillows, 8 aprons and 5 bundles, for $750.56 profit a week.
  • Piping is what limits profit. Each extra yard adds about $7.70 a week, while extra hours on their own add nothing, so we recommended buying piping in bulk.
  • Suggested simple sales tracking so the model can be re-run each quarter with real demand.
  • Excel Solver
  • Integer programming
  • Sensitivity analysis
  • Client interviews
